# Referee review block: figures

Referee report — figures and presentation only.

1. The set is broadly appropriate but not yet sufficient for the claim. F1 is the central figure; F3 is a needed ground-truth check; F5 translates error into sensitivity; F2 gives the statistical limit; F4 characterizes the parameter ensemble. Redundant: F1a and F5 both plot performance versus repetitions; if space is tight, combine or move F5 to Supplemental. F2b can duplicate F1 unless it answers a distinct bound-efficiency question. Missing: an explicit photon-budget axis, not merely repetitions; the per-column structure of the crossover; and evidence that the result is not one-session specific. If repetitions are proportional to detected photons, state the conversion and add a photon-number axis; otherwise the “photon-budget” claim is not directly shown. If the amortized network is central, justify its absence from F3/F5.

2. Several panels can mislead. In F1, percentile-bootstrap bars over the 34 columns are not independent experimental replications; they are column-to-column variability from one session/instrument. Do not present them as confidence intervals for the method unless the sampling unit and limitation are explicit. Mixing these with the network’s interquartile training-seed spread in the same bars is not defensible. Use separate encodings: solid curve = mean/median across columns; light band = column IQR; hatched band or thin ensemble = training-seed IQR. Label them “variability bands,” not error bars, unless multiple independent sessions/instruments exist. Reconcile 34 bootstrapped columns with the 40 shown in F4. F2b is also misleading if each estimator is normalized to its own bound: an estimator can appear efficient while remaining far above the stricter joint bound. Normalize to a common bound or indicate absolute RMSE. F4: with n=40, five histograms are weak and hard to read at 8.6 cm; use dot/strip plots or small multiples. For p, show how many values are saturated at 4.0 and state whether 4.0 is physical or numerical. For φ0, avoid arbitrary circular histogram binning or report circular mean resultant length.

3. The median/RMSE inconsistency is not acceptable as the primary comparison. RMSE is the metric tied to F1, F2 and CRB analysis, and exact synthetic truth makes RMSE computable. If median |dB| is shown to demonstrate robustness to outliers, include RMSE as the main curve or inset, and show quartiles. A caption alone does not make different risk metrics comparable.

4. Add one “crossover” panel, preferably Fig. 1c. Plot versus detected photons per delay point — or repetitions with a photon-number top axis — the ratio  
\[
\mathrm{RMSE_{pooled}}/\mathrm{RMSE_{per\text{-}trace}} .
\]
Show all columns as faint grey lines, the median as a thick black line, a horizontal line at unity, a vertical line at \(r^*\) with uncertainty, and the shaded lowest measured budget. Optionally add the amortized network as a dotted median line. This directly shows the sign change, column heterogeneity, and photon-budget location of the crossover; it is much more persuasive than aggregate RMSE curves alone.

5. Axis choices: if the floor law has power-law asymptotes, F1a should be log-log or at least log-x. Make the low-\(r\) slope and high-\(r\) floor visible; if the floor compresses the curves too much, use log-x/linear-y. F1b should use the same x-transform as F1a because the panels share an axis; signed bias belongs on a linear y scale with a zero line and, if useful, a band showing the statistical-error scale. The crossover annotation is not self-contained: label \(r^*=9\times10^4\) directly on the plot, give its uncertainty, use a line colour not used by data curves, and label the shaded strip as “lowest measured budget” adjacent to it. At 8.6 cm, annotations must be readable without zooming; legends should be consolidated, and colours/linestyles colour-blind safe.
